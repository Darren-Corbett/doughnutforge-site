_26 November 2026. The settings below were checked on this date; they change often._

**In short:** keep passwords, bank and card details, ID numbers, and private details about your health or other people out of AI chatbots. For everything else, a simple test from the UK's National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC) works well: do not type anything that would cause you a problem if it were made public.

Chatbots such as ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude and Copilot are good at drafting a letter, explaining a bill or planning a trip. None of that needs your most private details. This guide covers what to keep out, what each service says it keeps, and a few habits that take a minute to set up.

## Why it is worth a moment's thought

When you type into a chatbot, your words go to the company that runs it. The NCSC puts it plainly: your question "will be visible to the organisation providing" the service. Most services save your chats in a history. Some keep them for months or years, and some may use them to improve their AI unless you switch that off.

Google's Gemini help pages also say that some chats are looked at by trained reviewers "to help improve Google services", and ask: "Please don't enter confidential information that you wouldn't want a reviewer to see."

None of this means chatbots are unsafe to use. It means a chat is more like a conversation with a company than a private notebook, and it helps to choose what you say with that in mind.

## What to keep out

1. **Passwords, PINs and one-time codes.** A chatbot does not need them to help you with anything.
2. **Bank and card details.** Card numbers, security codes, account numbers and sort codes.
3. **ID numbers and documents.** Passport, driving licence and National Insurance numbers, and photos of the documents themselves.
4. **Health details with your name on them.** Asking what a word on a test result means is fine. Pasting the whole letter, with your name, address and NHS number, is not needed.
5. **Other people's private details.** A friend's health, a family matter, a neighbour's address. They have not chosen to share it.
6. **Confidential work material.** Client files, contracts, staff records, anything marked confidential. Many employers have their own rules on AI tools, so check those first.
7. **Your name, address, date of birth and phone number, all together.** These are the details banks and other services use to check that it is you, so it is safer to keep them in fewer places.

The NCSC's own advice is short: do not "include sensitive information in queries" to public AI chatbots, and do not ask anything "that would lead to issues were they made public".

## Ask the question without the private part

Most of the time the answer does not depend on your personal details. Before you paste something in, take them out or swap them for placeholders:

- Replace names with "[my name]" or "Person A".
- Replace account numbers, addresses and dates of birth with "[number]", "[address]" and "[date]".
- For a letter or a bill, type out only the sentence you want explained, rather than pasting or photographing the whole page. A photo shows everything on it, including the parts you did not mean to share.
- Describe the situation in general terms. "My bank has charged a fee I did not expect. What should I ask them?" works as well as the statement itself.

You still get the help, and the details stay with you.

## Three questions to ask of any chatbot

Every company that runs a chatbot publishes its own answers. Three questions matter most: does it keep your chats, for how long, and can you stop them being used to train its AI? This is what each company's own help pages said on 26 November 2026, for personal accounts. Work and school accounts often follow different rules.

### ChatGPT (OpenAI)

- **Keeps your chats:** Yes, in your chat history. They stay there even if you turn training off.
- **For how long:** The help page gives no period for ordinary chats. Temporary Chats "may be retained for up to 30 days for safety purposes".
- **Stop training:** Turn off **Improve the model for everyone** in **Settings > Data controls**. Temporary Chats "are not used to improve OpenAI models".

### Gemini (Google)

- **Keeps your chats:** Yes, in **Gemini History**, where you can review and delete them.
- **For how long:** 18 months by default, then deleted automatically; you can choose 3 months, 36 months or to keep it indefinitely. Chats seen by reviewers are kept for up to three years, even if you delete your history. With History off, chats are still kept for "around two months".
- **Stop training:** Use the **Chat content** setting, reached from Gemini History. With it off, future chats "won't be used to train generative AI models, unless you provide feedback".

### Claude (Anthropic)

- **Keeps your chats:** Yes, in your chat history, and you can delete any chat.
- **For how long:** A deleted chat leaves your history at once and Anthropic's storage "within 30 days". If you allow training, data may be kept "in a de-identified format for up to 5 years".
- **Stop training:** Turn off **Help Improve our AI models** in **Settings > Privacy**. Incognito chats "are not used to improve Claude".

### Microsoft Copilot (the current app)

- **Keeps your chats:** Yes, in the **Chats** list. Delete one with **More > Delete**, or all of them from the Microsoft privacy dashboard.
- **For how long:** The help page for the current app gives no period.
- **Stop training:** Microsoft's page says prompts, responses and files in the current Copilot app "aren't used to train foundation models".

Two things stand out. First, every service lets you delete your chats, and each one either lets you switch training off in a minute or says it does not train its main AI on your chats. Second, a deleted chat is not always gone at once, so the safest copy of a private detail is the one you never typed.

Menu names move from time to time. If a setting is not where this post says, search the company's help pages for the setting's name.

## Simple habits that help

1. **Switch off training if you prefer.** It takes a minute with the settings above. On ChatGPT, OpenAI says that when you are signed in, the setting applies to your account "across web and mobile devices", so you only need to do it once.
2. **Do not rate replies in chats that hold personal details.** A thumbs up or down can send that chat to be used for training, even with the setting off. OpenAI says that if you give feedback, "the entire conversation associated with that feedback may be used to train OpenAI models". Gemini's setting stops training "unless you provide feedback".
3. **Use a temporary chat for one-off questions.** ChatGPT has Temporary Chats, Gemini has temporary chats and Claude has Incognito chats. They are still kept for a short time (ChatGPT may keep them for up to 30 days, and Google keeps Gemini's for around two months), so the list of things to keep out still applies.
4. **Delete chats you no longer need.** A chat history that only holds what you are using is easier to keep tidy. Gemini also lets you choose how long your history is kept before it is deleted automatically.
5. **Check the answers that matter.** The NCSC notes that AI "can get things wrong and present incorrect statements as facts". For health, money or legal questions, use the chatbot to understand the words, then check with the NHS, your bank or a professional before you act.
6. **Think before you upload.** Files and photos count as much as typed words. Take out the private parts first, or type the question instead.

The same thinking works for the apps on your phone. [Should I delete apps for privacy?](https://doughnut-forge.app/blog/should-i-delete-apps-for-privacy/) shows how to check what an app collects and how to change what it is allowed to see.

## A quick test before you press send

Ask yourself: "Would I be comfortable if this appeared on a postcard?" If the answer is yes, go ahead. If not, take the private part out, or keep that question for a person you trust. Used this way, a chatbot can be a helpful tool that never needs to know who you are.
